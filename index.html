How Keepers Work
No Year Limit — Just Escalating Cost
Keepers don't expire after a set number of years. A player stays keeper-eligible for as long as his owner wants him and he still qualifies — the only thing that changes year to year is what he costs. Each team can carry up to 2 keepers at a time.
How the Keeper Cost Escalates
  • A drafted player can be kept at one round better than where he was picked (e.g. drafted Rd 8 → costs Rd 7 to keep).
  • Keep him again the following year and the cost jumps 2 rounds instead of 1 — marked with an * in the original spreadsheet. Every year kept after that keeps jumping the same way.
  • Players originally drafted in rounds 3–5 are capped at a 1st-round keeper cost no matter how far the math would otherwise push them.
  • Players drafted in round 1 or 2 the prior year can't be kept that first offseason.
The Keeper Cost column isn't computed live on this page — it's entered by the commissioner right after each draft using the rules above. If a number here looks off, it's a data entry to fix, not a rule to debug.
How We Score Keeper Value
The Value tag compares a player's keeper round against his ADP — where he'd actually go in a fresh startup draft today. ADP here comes from KeepTradeCut's crowdsourced Superflex / .5 PPR dynasty rankings, converted into a round.pick using this league's 14 teams (rank 15 → the 2nd pick of round 2, etc.). The difference between the two, in rounds, is the whole story: keep him at round 11 when he'd actually go in round 1, and that's a 10-round discount. Keep him at round 1 when his real ADP is also round 1, and you've gained nothing — you could've just drafted him fresh and used the keeper slot on someone actually cheap.
  • Elite Value — 5+ rounds cheaper than his ADP.
  • Good Value — 3–4 rounds cheaper.
  • Fair Value — 1–2 rounds cheaper.
  • No Real Edge — roughly break-even with his ADP.
  • Overpay — costs more than his ADP says he's worth. Common for aging veterans whose keeper cost hasn't caught up to their decline.
This is also why a round-one keeper cost rarely pays off: his ADP is very likely round one too, so there's no discount to be had. Players without an ADP (outside KTC's top 500 — mostly deep bench and D/ST) show no Value tag rather than a guess.
