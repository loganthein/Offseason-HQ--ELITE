How Keepers Work
Keepers Are Capped at 2 Seasons
A player can be kept for up to 2 seasons before he goes back into the draft pool — after that, whichever team wants him has to redraft him like anyone else. This cap applies to normal keepers and the new free-agent keeper rule below; it does not apply to a Designated Player (see below), since that's a one-time, single-season tag to begin with. Each team can still carry up to 2 keepers at a time, separate from a possible Designated Player slot.
How the Keeper Cost Escalates
  • A drafted player can be kept at one round better than where he was picked (e.g. drafted Rd 8 → costs Rd 7 to keep).
  • Keep him again the following year and the cost jumps 2 rounds instead of 1 — marked with an * in the original spreadsheet. That second kept year is also his last one under the 2-season cap above.
  • Players originally drafted in rounds 3–5 are capped at a 1st-round keeper cost no matter how far the math would otherwise push them.
  • Players drafted in round 1 or 2 the prior year can't be kept that first offseason.
The Keeper Cost column isn't computed live on this page — it's entered by the commissioner right after each draft using the rules above. If a number here looks off, it's a data entry to fix, not a rule to debug.
Free-Agent Pickups Can Now Be Kept
A player added off waivers/free agency — never drafted or kept — is now keeper-eligible too. His first year as a keeper costs a flat 6th-round pick, then follows the escalation rules above (one round tighter the next year, then jumping 2 rounds) for as long as the 2-season cap allows. This uses one of a team's 2 normal keeper slots, same as any other keeper.
Designated Player — a One-Time 3rd Keeper Slot
Once per player, a team can tag him as their Designated Player for a single season, keeping him at a flat 10th-round cost regardless of what he'd normally cost. This is a 3rd keeper slot on top of a team's normal 2 — it doesn't use one of those spots. Because it's a one-time, single-season tag, it isn't subject to the 2-season keeper cap above — but a given player can only ever be someone's Designated Player once. Kept again after that, he's a normal keeper (one of the 2 slots, standard escalation, 2-season cap).

Side Games

Hammer, Hot Potato, and Survivor — computed from this season's weekly results once they exist. Here's what each one is in the meantime.

🔨 Hammer — Week 1's top scorer wins it. The only way to take it is to beat the current holder head-to-head. Whoever's holding it at season's end wins it outright.
🥕 Hot Potato — Week 1's low scorer gets stuck with it. The only way to ditch it: win your matchup, and whoever you just beat inherits it. Don't be holding it at season's end.
🏆 Survivor — Lowest score each week is eliminated (ties broken by bench points). Top score each week gets immunity the following week. The second-to-last week cuts the field to exactly two finalists; the higher score in the final week wins it all.
